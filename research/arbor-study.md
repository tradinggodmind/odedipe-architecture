# Belgrade Arbor - spatial identity as a digital experience

Study date 7 November 2026. Primary sources are the live English website, its residences and 3D routes, and Square43's published project account. Desktop browser observations complement the source study. The original designers' stated rationale is distinguished from our interpretation.

## What the experience is selling

Arbor presents one property development with several purchase paths. Its first job is emotional recognition. Its second is to help a prospective buyer find an actual residence or commercial unit. The site therefore alternates atmosphere with selection tools. The brand belongs to the building itself, while Michael's brand belongs to the person creating images across fifteen buildings. That distinction changes the navigation and information hierarchy of the portfolio.

## The first viewport

The observed desktop hero fills the viewport with a near-frontal architectural scene. A fine, high-contrast serif wordmark occupies much of the lower frame. The lower image is darkened, preserving readable light lettering without placing it on an opaque card. A narrow floating navigation capsule sits near the top centre, leaving the corners and the building's silhouette open.

The opening sequence initially showed a cream loading field, a gold symbol, a fine progress rule and a numeric percentage. The browser then revealed the image and title. This makes loading part of the identity. It also delays access to the work, and therefore is not carried into Michael's site. A visitor to a visualiser's portfolio should reach a recognisable image immediately.

At the observed 1363px browser width, the hero H1 computed to about143px with equal line height. Major section type computed to about53px with58px leading. The menu label was about13px. These are responsive computed values, not fixed universal specifications. The large difference between display type and small controls creates hierarchy without many font weights.

## Palette and type

Square43 identifies the serif as PP Old Style and the supporting face as Work Sans. The live page's CSS family alias for the serif is editorialOld. These labels should not be treated as permission to redistribute the original font. Our portfolio uses the independently sourced open-licensed Instrument Serif and Work Sans.

The published system includes pine green#2E3A26, gold#856740, navy#314759, green#60735C, cream#FFF4D6, sand#F4F0DB, vanilla#FDFBEF and acid yellow#E7FD55. The observed navigation uses a deep translucent green field, light sand lettering and a restrained gold identity mark. Color is assigned by role. The most saturated tone is an accent, not a blanket treatment across photographs.

Our inference is that this warm, muted system makes glass and concrete feel residential and natural. The type adds an editorial tone. The contrast comes from a soft image atmosphere against a precise interface, rather than from many ornamental effects.

## Architecture supplies the graphic language

The design team's own account connects the identity to tree-canopy geometry in the facade. Faceted shapes, angular folds and masks extend that architectural motif into the website. This is a particularly useful lesson: a memorable visual device has a reason to exist when it originates in the subject.

For Michael, a literal canopy motif would over-identify the portfolio with one reference. The transferable principle becomes a repeated relationship between expressive geometry and measured information. Curved roofs and towers supply the image character. Fine rules, project numbers and aligned captions supply the interface structure.

## Navigation and state

The desktop capsule has three anchors: menu on the left, the identity at centre, and enquiry on the right. Opening it expands the capsule into a large rounded panel over the hero. The page remains visible behind the translucent field. Large centred serif links provide the main routes, with language options at the base.

This preserves orientation and reduces permanent interface weight. The contact action remains visible before the menu opens. The tradeoff is discoverability for users who do not recognise the compressed menu. A portfolio has fewer top-level destinations, so Michael's index needs only Work, Approach, About and Contact. Its native dialog provides keyboard focus management and Escape dismissal.

The live Arbor navigation separates Home, 3D Experience, Residences, Business, Retail, Location, Amenities, Contact and Investor information. This is a sales architecture. Michael's project pages instead separate the image sequence, concept story, material treatment and drawing evidence. That difference keeps the portfolio clear about the service being offered.

## Page rhythm and content roles

The home page moves from a hero through architectural identity and living proposition, then location, residences, business and retail, lifestyle, amenities, an elevated view, payment stages and contact. The repetition of photographic scenes builds an imagined daily experience around the property. The practical information appears after the emotional world has been established.

Several location items carry nearby-place context and travel times. These are functional reassurance devices for a buyer. They are not useful additions to a visualiser's portfolio. Michael's equivalent reassurance comes from image continuity, a clear view of the interiors, specific material captions and readable drawings.

The scroll experience also repeats indexed sequences and large editorial phrases. The indices make a long narrative feel organised. In the portfolio, the same principle becomes fifteen numbered studies and six numbered images per project, so the visitor can judge both breadth and depth.

## Interactive property selection

The residences route exposes bedroom, area and floor-based filtering, plus outside-space characteristics. Square43 describes a Three.js experience with floor and unit selection, finish comparison and day/night views. These controls have a direct property-selection purpose. The designer account names Next.js and Three.js for the implementation.

A portfolio of finished still images does not require an interactive model. Simulating one with arbitrary image motion would imply functionality that is absent. Michael's category filter and full-screen gallery provide real navigation value with little runtime work. Each drawing remains available as a vector PDF, preserving detail without loading every full document on arrival.

## Rebuilding the visible language

A faithful visual rebuild would require a responsive shell, matching typography and licensing, an image sequence with equivalent crops, the capsule's closed and open geometry, section masks, an indexed scroll narrative and the correct selection states. It would also require all model assets and a tested state model for a fully equivalent 3D experience. Screenshots alone cannot reveal unit data, backend logic or the author's unpublished decisions.

Implement the capsule as a true button controlling a labelled navigation region. Define closed/open states before adding motion. Animate opacity and transforms where possible. Use clipping only where the motif makes it necessary. Ensure the content remains navigable when motion is reduced. A loading indicator should reflect real readiness and have an error exit, rather than hide an unresolved asset forever.

## Limits of the observation

This was a visual and public-source study, not a measured real-user performance audit. Some media elements reported unavailable playback in the text accessibility surface while the static composition rendered. No claim is made that every media stream or apartment state was exercised. Mobile proportions are not inferred from desktop screenshots. Where the design team's published explanation supplies a fact, it is identified as such; other design-purpose statements are analysis.
